Cloud seeding in Colombia

Who decides when and where clouds are seeded in Colombia, and who lives with the rain that falls, or doesn't fall, as a result?

41 · Camilo · started 2026-11-09

In 1956 and 1957, W. E. Howell Associates ran a campaign against windstorms in the banana plantations near Santa Marta, on the Caribbean coast of Colombia (López and Howell, 1961). The map below is the study zone from their article, laid over the weather of 1956 as ERA5 reconstructs it.

Situate

The zone runs from the Magdalena river and Barranquilla in the west to the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta and the César river in the east, with the banana zone on the Sierra's western slopes.

Sources and limits

  • Study zone map: archival figure (cartographic) from Manuel E. López and Wallace E. Howell, “The Campaign Against Windstorms in the Banana Plantations Near Santa Marta, Colombia, 1956–57”, Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society 42, no. 4 (April 1961): 265. © American Meteorological Society. Georeferenced by Camilo (Web Mercator). Colours inverted here: paper black, lines white. Permission to publish: to check with the AMS.
  • Weather: ERA5 hourly reanalysis (reanalysed), ECMWF / Copernicus Climate Change Service, served by the Open-Meteo historical weather API (models=era5, no terrain correction). Licence: CC BY 4.0. Grid 0.25° (about 28 km); the model itself runs at about 31 km. Every 6 hours through 1956: rain is the 6-hour total, gust the 6-hour maximum, the rest the value at that moment. Nothing is smoothed. Download date and details: data/era5-1956.json and tools/fetch_era5.py.
  • Basemap: © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), standard tiles from tile.openstreetmap.org, turned grey and inverted here (grey and darkened in 3D).
  • Terrain (Simulate v2): Re:Earth Terrain, terrain.reearth.land (code MIT), tileset “mapterhorn-egm08”: the Mapterhorn global elevation model, a blend of several public elevation datasets (CC BY 4.0), with EGM2008 geoid heights (NGA). Elevation is measured from space in recent decades, not in 1956. Which datasets make up Mapterhorn here, and their resolution: to check. Tiles are loaded live; detail grows as you zoom in and averages out peaks from far away (the Sierra shows about 5,340 m, the real summit about 5,700 m). Heights are true scale.
  • 3D view: CesiumJS 1.146.0 (Apache-2.0), loaded from the jsDelivr CDN. The sky layer sits 5,000 m above the WGS 84 ellipsoid, within about 10 m of sea level here.

What this sketch cannot show: where or when clouds were actually seeded, or whether seeding changed anything. ERA5 for 1956 rests on few observations (no weather satellites, few stations in this region), and its rainfall is the model's own calculation, not measured rain. One ERA5 square is wider than the banana zone, so storms, valleys and the slopes of the Sierra are averaged inside each square.
