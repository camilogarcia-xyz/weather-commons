Cloud seeding in Colombia

Who decides when and where clouds are seeded in Colombia, and who lives with the rain that falls, or doesn't fall, as a result?

41 · Camilo · started 2026-11-09

In 1956 and 1957, W. E. Howell Associates ran a campaign against windstorms in the banana plantations near Santa Marta, on the Caribbean coast of Colombia (López and Howell, 1961). The map below is the study zone from their article, laid over the weather of 1956 as ERA5 reconstructs it.

Map: archival figure (cartographic), López and Howell 1961, inverted. Squares: ERA5 (reanalysed), one per 0.25° cell, not smoothed. Basemap: OpenStreetMap and CARTO.

Situate

The zone runs from the Magdalena river and Barranquilla in the west to the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta and the César river in the east, with the banana zone on the Sierra's western slopes.

Georeferenced by Camilo (Web Mercator). Colours inverted here: paper black, lines white. Permission to publish: to check with the AMS.

Grid 0.25° (about 28 km); the model itself runs at about 31 km. Every 6 hours through 1956: rain is the 6-hour total, gust the 6-hour maximum, the rest the value at that moment. Nothing is smoothed. Download date and details: data/era5-1956.json and tools/fetch_era5.py.
  • Basemap: © OpenStreetMap contributors, © CARTO (dark tiles, made lighter grey here).

What this sketch cannot show: where or when clouds were actually seeded, or whether seeding changed anything. ERA5 for 1956 rests on few observations (no weather satellites, few stations in this region), and its rainfall is the model's own calculation, not measured rain. One ERA5 square is wider than the banana zone, so storms, valleys and the slopes of the Sierra are averaged inside each square.
